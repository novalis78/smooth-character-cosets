## What the decimals establish

Write c* = C(1)⁻². The manuscript proves its smooth-number estimate under RH for the relevant character for every fixed η > c*. If y = η(log p)², a number is y-smooth when every one of its prime factors is at most y. For the quadratic character the estimate means that the residue and nonresidue shares of these integers up to x both tend to one half, uniformly for pᵃ ≤ x ≤ p. Carneiro et al. give c* < 0.7615. Lopin's versioned witness gives c* < 0.760777412. The first bound suffices for the illustrative cutoff 0.77. The second permits 0.7608 too. Neither numerical bound on its own proves that the shares approach one half. Each fixes how small an example one can give in the manuscript's theorem. The rate and onset in the theorem can depend on the chosen fixed η.

For y below the first quadratic nonresidue, all primes up to y have character +1. Every product of such primes then has character +1, by multiplicativity. Hence the signed smooth count is exactly the unsigned count, including the integer 1. Where the relative error bound tends to zero, this equality is impossible for sufficiently large p. This gives a necessary condition on any successful cutoff. The optimum equidistribution cutoff is unknown. [Carneiro et al., Theorems 1, 4 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/4154) · [Lopin's versioned certificate](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24408857)

## Hand review of the analytic estimates

For Lemma 1, translating the test by t multiplies its Fourier transform by exp(−2πitv). At v = log(n)/(2π) this produces precisely χ(n)n⁻ⁱᵗ on the prime side. The test's factor 1/2 and the two conjugate prime terms give the factor 1/(2π) in the weighted sum. The bound on the zeros integrates the translated envelope |F(v−t)|, whose derivative in absolute value is at most |F′(v−t)| almost everywhere. This avoids a spurious factor from differentiating the oscillating modulation. Combining the positive ordinates for χ with the reflected ordinates for its conjugate counts a central zero with its full multiplicity. For |t| ≤ exp(log p/log y), the log(3+|t|) term is o(log p). This is the substantive uniform estimate for which a specialist's review is particularly valuable. [Carneiro et al., §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08380v2#S3) · [Carneiro, Chandee and Milinovich, Theorem 5 and Example 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.00955)

At the saddle, y⁻ᵅ is asymptotic to (log x)/(2y). Multiplication by the lower bound for the sum of the phase deficits over primes therefore gives an Euler-product loss proportional to u = (log x)/(log y). The last section of `fourier/cutoff_077_derivation.md` gives a second proof of passage to the literal count n ≤ x, including integer endpoints, conditional on the Euler-product and saddle estimates. For the ordinary sum, `ordinary-audit.md` checks the moving integration line and Gaussian smoothing and gives a separate calculation of the worst-case exponent.

The full analytic theorem and priority are outside the Lean and arithmetic certificates.
